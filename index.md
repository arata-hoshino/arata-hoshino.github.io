Most writing about software describes how systems are supposed to work. This series is about
what happens afterwards: when the load arrives, when the assumptions age, and when the
diagram on the whiteboard meets the traffic on Monday morning.

The essays are meant to be read in order, though each one stands on its own. They share a
single argument: **the hard part of engineering is rarely the code**. It is the accumulated
weight of decisions that were reasonable in isolation and unbearable in combination.

Everything here is written in plain Markdown, kept in version control, and published as static
HTML. There is no database and no editor — a new essay is a new file in `_essays/`.
