If what you have read so far has looked like a work of optimism, this chapter should correct that. This thesis is optimistic about supply and pessimistic about distribution. To put it precisely, the prediction here is that the redistribution of wealth will fail once, somewhere in the 2030s. And the first half of that prediction, the leftward turn in politics, has already begun.

## 6.1 The invention of the task

Our ancestors invented the task as the instrument by which wealth is distributed. Work was broken into discrete tasks, and income has been allocated through the performance of those tasks. The system worked because differentiated skill determined who could perform which task, and the value of the task a person could perform determined their income. Do X and you are paid Y. Reduced to its essentials, distribution in the modern economy runs on that one line.

That line began to fail before AI. On Karabarbounis and Neiman's well-known measurement,<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup> the labor share, meaning the portion of created value that goes to labor, has fallen significantly since the early 1980s across the majority of countries and industries. About half of that fall is explained by machines and computers getting relatively cheaper and firms replacing people with them. On the theoretical side, Acemoglu and Restrepo's task-based model identified the mechanism.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup> The displacement effect, by which automation hands a human task over to a machine, always lowers the labor share. The only thing that can offset it is the reinstatement effect, in which new tasks are created for humans and labor comes back. In the United States of recent years, displacement accelerated and reinstatement weakened. On their estimate, 50 to 70 percent of the change in the American wage structure is explained by task displacement.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>

The problem is not the displacement of tasks but distribution. The automation of tasks is not new; it has run continuously since the industrial revolution. In that revolution, cheap labor and cheap energy moved workers out of agriculture and into manufacturing. Human skill stayed adaptable and stayed meaningful. What is different in the age of AI is that automation can remove the need for human labor itself, not task by task but across whole categories of work: displacement spreads across every kind of job while there is no sign of new human tasks appearing. The new industrial revolution will be driven by cheap automated labor and cheap fusion energy, and by the time it arrives the machinery of distribution will have gone first.
