## 9.1 Recap

The energy and the population that advanced countries have given up on moving do move in the Gulf. The talent that money is supposed to move is the one that moves least. That inversion was the starting point.

This thesis set out three terms: institutions, the effective abundance of energy, and labor input, with labor input splitting further into deep tech and large capital. Because it is a product, if one term is near zero the output is near zero. What it settles is not affluence but the capacity to build. The effective abundance of energy is measured by price, by reliability, and by connection lead time.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup>

The efficiency of a civilization is the process by which two convergence costs, the placement of matter and prediction, fall toward zero. The two compete on the same problem and the cheaper side wins. For two hundred years the speed of automation has been set by energy and capital goods, and it is set by them this time too.

For fifty years the advanced countries rebuilt their economies, their institutions and their values on the premise that energy does not grow. The scarcity was chosen; only the awareness of choosing it was missing. It struck households through the prices of the Baumol sectors and of housing, and it suppressed births. The Malthusian trap did not disappear. Energy took the seat food used to occupy.

What follows the lifting of a constraint is not a discount but an explosion of demand. But of the four head-counts an economy requires, automation bypasses only labor supply; the other three are population itself. Even on the most optimistic assumptions, the valley of demand lasts half a century from 2035.

What raises the floor of that valley is distribution. The conduit that allocates income by contribution to tasks has already begun to break, and neither a single country's wealth tax nor a cash transfer changes who owns the capital. Taxation settles the quantity of distribution; ownership settles its route. And once use value can be observed directly, price is no longer needed as its proxy.

The unit is the cluster, not the nation, and no cluster holds every term on its own. Foundation models and basic research in AI are in America, the advanced physical-technology supply chain is in Japan, Korea and Taiwan, and joules, land, large capital and speed of decision are in the Gulf. Whoever integrates those three regions under one vision will settle where the physical base of the later twenty-first century sits.

Stagnation, rising prices, the failure of distribution and falling births are several symptoms of one constraint. So one prescription is enough. Put the missing term within reach.

## 9.2 What to invest in

Down from the argument to practice. If the readings so far are correct, the allocation of resources from here takes the following order.

**One. Energy R&D.** This has been this thesis's top priority throughout, and it does not change here either. The bottleneck is neither models nor capital but the speed at which gigawatts are connected to the grid. And the lead time for a new grid connection is now the most powerful instrument of industrial policy in the world. A policy that shortens the queue moves the ranking of clusters faster than a policy that lowers the price. That speed is measured not by the capacity sitting in the queue but by the proportion of the queue that reached energization, by the share under firm contract, and by the grid operator's willingness to name a connection date.

**Two. Implementation design for the physical world.** Palo Alto makes AI's brain and Northeast Asia makes its body. But the people who design what, concretely, that does in a factory, a hospital, a city, logistics and agriculture are decisively short. This is not an engineer's question but a design question. In the language of Chapter 2, it is the work of deciding where to apply the lever, once direction 2 has become the lever that collapses the development cost of direction 1. Implementation design will be the profession in thinnest supply over the coming decade.

**Three. Composing the terms between capital and technology.** This is not brokerage but composing, in both languages, which term capital goes into, on what horizon, and on what conditions. Only capital that can read the function of Chapter 1 gets placed in the right place.

**Four. Institutions of distribution.** The birth-endowment capital accounts, the citizen-share sovereign fund and the universal share in automation capital argued in Chapter 6. This is not an item of ethics but an item of engineering that settles whether a cluster is still functioning ten years from now. The cluster that implements it seriously first buys the social stability of the 2040s.

**Five. Basic investment in the side outside the stack.** In a world where supply is automated the scarce thing is demand. And for the half-century from 2035, the only part of the world where head-count is rising is the part now placed outside the stack. As section 8.9 of Chapter 8 showed, the twentieth-century development model will not be replayed, so that head-count does not turn into purchasing power on its own. If the cascade is real, then fresh water, electricity and education can arrive there as goods whose marginal cost has approached zero, and the societies now paying the highest effective prices are the first and largest market for those goods. Directing capital there is not charity for the side crossing the half-century valley of demand but the creation of demand. If capital does not go into designs that reach that market, this thesis's argument ends as an in-house conversation on the frontier.

Of the five, the first already offers positions that can be taken today, without waiting for any technology to be finished, because what becomes scarce after the constraint comes off can be identified before it does.

One is land already connected to the grid. The median period from application to entry into operation, for projects entering operation in the United States in 2025, was 61 months, and of the projects applied for between 2000 and 2020 only 13 percent by capacity reached operation at all.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup> A new interconnection right is no longer bought with time; it is bought with probability. What sits on the site of a decommissioned thermal plant, or on the grounds of a works running at low utilization, is not a building but a right that is no longer issued on the same terms. The return exists, though, because the procedure is slow. If the first priority is actually achieved, the scarcity thins, and whoever holds that land stands to lose from the reform he ought to be pushing.

The other is the manufacturing slot for equipment. The delivery time for large transformers went from under a year before the pandemic to a standard three years, with generator step-up transformers at 144 weeks.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-3">3</a></sup> The procedure can be shortened; the transformer's delivery time cannot. So the queue that matters is not only the one on the grid but the one at the factory, and what is there to be taken is the slot itself, and one layer below it the copper, the grain-oriented electrical steel and the people who can wind a coil. The same logic applies to physical AI. When humanoids enter volume production, what runs out first is not the finished machine but the capacity to make actuators, precision motors and thermal management.

## 9.3 How to invest

How capital is placed matters as much as where, because a position taken against an existing bottleneck solves none of the problems argued in Chapter 6.

The answer of Chapter 8 was not to pick one cluster but to ride the division of the terms itself. As Figure 8-1 shows, no place carries every term at world level; and if that is so, the return is not in the places that have assembled the terms but on the routes that connect one term to another.

Here is how the loop closes. The money comes from the Gulf states, and above all from the sovereign wealth of the UAE, which holds large capital and effective energy abundance at the world's highest level and stands on the buying side of deep tech. That money goes to the side that holds deep tech: on the bits side the foundation models and AI basic research of the San Francisco Bay Area, Texas and BosWash; on the physical side the leading-edge physical supply chain of Japan-Korea-Taiwan, and the next generation of nuclear reactors. Most of what is invested in is startups, but large companies holding a technology, individuals holding an idea and the IP to it, and university and corporate laboratories and studios are all the same target. If the counterparty holds a term, the legal form it arrives in does not matter. And then those same parties choose the UAE as the place to run their physical experiments. Energy is cheap and there is no need to watch the meter, with not only fossil fuel but effectively unlimited sunlight. Regulation is light and land is spare. Whether a technology that has left the laboratory works on top of an actual city can only be found out where those three conditions are assembled at once, which is what makes Dubai and Abu Dhabi the places on earth where implementation goes furthest.

The body that turns this loop supplies capital and terms at once. It puts in capital as a venture fund, stands up companies that do not yet exist alongside their founders as a studio, and brings the energy, compute and regulatory parties into one contract as a consortium. For as long as three separate bodies do those three things, the terms never come together. When one body does all three at once, what results can be called an ecosystem.

What it offers is more than money. It guarantees a gigawatt-scale grid position and an allocation of compute from the outset, conditional on the demonstration running on its site, so that what a founder cannot obtain anywhere else is written into the terms of founding. A fund buys into companies that already exist. What is needed here is to design companies that do not exist yet, together with their infrastructure.

What it can offer in exchange is effective energy abundance and the speed of decision. As section 8.5 showed, the GCC holds those two and lacks only the coefficient on talent, while Japan, Korea, Taiwan and England hold the coefficient on talent and lack the energy. Putting the GCC's energy and the talent of Japan, Korea, Taiwan and England into one contract is what composing the terms means. Capital leaves from the side of energy and large capital, enters the side of deep tech, and the implementation comes back to the side of energy and institutions. That loop belongs, for now, to no one. I believe this model works.

## 9.4 The predictions

**One. Energy.** By the middle of the 2030s, in at least one major economic bloc, there will be a year in which new capital investment in nuclear fission exceeds new capital investment in renewables. Fusion will achieve its first commercial delivery of power to the grid in the 2030s, but the main theater of price impact is the diffusion phase in which the learning curve operates, which is to say the 2040s. The start-up of the first commercial reactor will be reported as the news that energy has become free. That report will be wrong.

**Two. Population.** China's annual births will not come off their structurally declining path, whatever official support measures are put in place, because the population of candidate mothers is already fixed. And the first place a statistically significant reversal in the birth rate is observed will not be a country with generous family policy. It will be a country, or a city, that has structurally brought down the effective price of energy and housing. The correlation between the size of the subsidy and the birth rate will weaken further over the coming decade.

**Three. Politics.** In the 2030s, in at least two major advanced economies, a wealth tax or a top marginal rate at the 70 percent level will appear in the platform of a party in government. Where it is implemented, it will be rolled back within five years by the movement of capital and of people. The first half of this prediction has already begun, in New York and Seattle in 2025.

**Four. Distribution.** In the same 2030s, institutions for making every citizen an investor, meaning capital accounts opened at birth, sovereign funds held as a national stake, and a universal stake in automated capital, will appear as mainstream policy in more than one country. This is the beginning of what this thesis calls the update to capitalism. On my reading, the first country to implement it in earnest buys itself the social stability of the 2040s.

**Five. Labor.** The relative fall in youth employment in the occupations most exposed to AI will keep widening through the second half of the 2020s, and in at least one major economic bloc youth unemployment will become the central issue in politics. The first wave of solutions offered at that point will be job retraining, and it will not work, because the reinstatement of tasks does not happen.

## 9.5 What would falsify this

Writing the predictions down is not enough. What would make me admit that one of them was wrong has to be written down to the same precision. The purpose of this table is not to protect the predictions but to keep them killable.<sup class="noteref"><a href="#note-4">4</a></sup>

What breaks in the skeleton of this thesis when one of them is falsified? If one is lost, the timeline of Chapter 5 slides backwards, but the identification of the constraint survives. If two is lost, the central proposition of Chapter 4 is itself wrong. If three and four are lost, the design argument of Chapter 6 was necessary and yet politically impossible. If five is lost, the erosion of the conduit, which is the premise of Chapter 6, was never happening in the first place. The most fragile is two. The one I least want to break is four. Some of these will be wrong, but the way they go wrong is the information. I intend to keep updating this reading by collision with reality.

## 9.6 What is not established here

Four things this thesis asserts and does not establish.

The link between energy prices, technological structure and the birth rate is set out as a mechanism, not measured as one; settling it takes a long-run panel of energy prices, generation mix, real wages and fertility across the advanced economies. The decomposition of the price system is qualitative; what is missing is an estimate, across housing, education, childcare and healthcare, of how much each cost driver contributes to the household Malthus, energy and non-automated labor above all. The account of urban growth rests on historical cases rather than on a model in which cheap energy, cheap labor and abundant land endogenously settle infrastructure investment and agglomeration. And the effect of automation on hours of work and on fertility behavior is argued rather than modeled: what shorter hours, redistributed income and reduced anxiety about the future do to the decision to marry and to have a child belongs in a household macro model this thesis does not build.

## 9.7 Past this, people decide

This thesis has restated, from one layer down, things that are usually discussed as questions of values or of policy: energy, computing capacity, technology, the price system, the household constraint. But the restatement has an end point. What geology and physics settle is whether the raw material of energy is there at all. What turns it into energy that is very nearly free, or leaves it in the ground, is institutions. Physics explains as far as institutions and no further. Past that, people decide.

If this reading is right, we become the first generation in human history to design the far side of the constraint rather than the inside of it.

I intend to spend my working life on making that co-creation happen. It is not a thing one person can assemble, so the design goes out first.

Let us meet and talk.
