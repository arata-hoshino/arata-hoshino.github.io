## 9.1 What to invest in

Which technologies and which institutions should capital go into, starting tomorrow, for the society on the far side of the constraint to arrive? The places map onto the terms of the equation in Chapter 1. Because it is a product, capital does most where the lowest term is. A second test: as section 5.7 showed, the only returns that can be relied on are the ones that do not depend on head-count, and businesses whose returns are proportional to the volume of demand sit in a structural headwind for the next half-century.

### Raising the effective abundance of energy

- **Fusion and SMRs.** Fusion at the top; conventional large reactors and small modular reactors (SMRs) built in series in a factory govern the transition. Renewables ride on top of firm power as a supplement and do not fill this term on their own. What sets the price is not discovery but volume. The cost curve follows Wright's law, and the contest turns on how many copies of one design get built. So what is worth buying is less the demonstration reactor itself than the production line and the supply chain that repeat one design after it. The effect on price arrives in the 2040s, and only capital that can wait that long belongs here. - **Grid infrastructure and heavy electrical equipment.** However much generation is added, nothing moves unless a new load can be connected to the grid. Three things are buyable: manufacturing capacity for transformers and switchgear, construction capacity for transmission lines and substations, and land already connected to the grid.
